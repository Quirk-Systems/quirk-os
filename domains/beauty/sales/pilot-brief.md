# Taste Engine Design-Partner Pilot

**Status:** candidate; do not sell as a proven product yet.

## Commercial hypothesis

People who make repeated beauty decisions will value recommendations more when they can see how their own explicit choices shaped the result—and when they retain control over what the system remembers.

## First participant

One committed test user who:

- can compare two beauty options;
- can test one recommendation in the real world;
- will explicitly record the outcome;
- will inspect the proposed graph change rather than rubber-stamping it.

This participant proves the chain, not the market.

## Problem statement

Most beauty recommendation experiences jump from broad questionnaire answers or observed behavior to a product suggestion. They rarely show the evidence path, distinguish purchase from satisfaction, or give the person control over learned preference state.

## Pilot promise

> Make one beauty choice. See what Quirk thinks it learned. Test the recommendation. Decide what—if anything—Quirk is allowed to remember.

## Pilot deliverables

- one bounded choice session;
- one explained recommendation;
- one real-world test;
- one explicit outcome record;
- one approve/revise/reject graph review;
- one evidence receipt;
- one debrief.

## Success evidence

- chain verifier passes;
- participant understands why the recommendation appeared;
- participant can identify and correct at least one misinterpretation if present;
- no unauthorized mutation occurs;
- participant states whether the experience would improve a repeated beauty decision.

## Kill conditions

Kill or redesign the wedge when:

- the participant cannot distinguish choice evidence from stable preference;
- the recommendation adds no value beyond the original choice;
- graph review feels like paperwork with no meaningful control;
- the outcome cannot be tested honestly;
- the system needs uninspectable model inference to seem useful;
- the only compelling metric is purchase conversion.
